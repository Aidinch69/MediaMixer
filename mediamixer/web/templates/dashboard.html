🌬️ Living ScreenBreathing tiles, slow-motion moments, double exposures, audio-reactive spirals.
What does this do?

Breathing Tiles: every tile slowly drifts and zooms a few percent on its own cycle, faster or slower with the Shared Pulse. Slow-Mo Moments: at high-energy moments one tile eases into slow motion for a couple of seconds with a motion-trail ghost, a soft push-in and a vignette, then eases back - it only picks a tile with plenty already buffered, so it never coincides with a real stall. Double Exposure: now and then a second clip fades in over a tile as a light blend, then separates. Smart Framing: each tile pans gently toward where the movement is in its clip (from Clip DNA), following it as it moves, with a slight zoom for room - like a slow camera operator. Colour Mood: the screen-edge glow, Light Leaks and Duotone palettes follow how warm or cool the clips on screen are (from Clip DNA), shifting gradually - jackpot flashes keep their own rotating colours. Spirals React to Audio: hits in the overlay track (or tile audio) give the spirals a quick swell - needs Audio-Reactive FX on. Fine-tune amounts and timing in Master Controls → Living Screen.

🎞️ Visual TexturesSplit frames, film grain & dust, edge bleed.
What does this do?

Split Frames: now and then one tile splits into two or four panes, each showing the same clip at a different moment, for a few seconds - the panes only appear once they're all loaded and in place, so it never looks like buffering. Film Grain & Dust: a light film grain over the screens with the odd speck of dust - barely there normally, stronger in calm moments (Vault zone, a Drift session, a wind-down, a Breath). Edge Bleed: each tile's colour glows softly into the gaps around it, so the grid feels like one picture. Fine-tune in Master Controls → Visual Textures.

🪝 Hooks & TeaseA clip's best bit repeats like a song's hook; some clips arrive pushed in close.
What does this do?

Hooks: when a clip reaches its best bit - where you've hearted it, or the strongest repeating stretch Clip DNA found - that couple of seconds plays three times before the clip carries on, the way a song repeats its hook. If the clip has a steady rhythm, the stretch is a whole number of its own beats so it repeats in time. Each repeat is ready before it's shown, so it never looks like buffering. Tease Reveal: now and then a new clip arrives pushed in close on where the action is, holds there a moment, then slowly pulls back to the full frame. Fine-tune in Master Controls → Hooks & Tease.

🧲 Crop Focus & GravityMosaic crops toward the action; the liveliest tile swells a little.
What does this do?

Mosaic Fit now tries every way of splitting the tiles into rows (not just even rows) and keeps the one that needs the least cropping - the line above shows how much is cut now versus the old way. Crop Focus: what Mosaic does have to cut off is taken from the empty side, not the action - each tile's crop slides toward where the movement is (from Clip DNA), gently. Gravity: the tile where the most is happening swells a little over its neighbours. It follows the movement in the clips; a heart only nudges it slightly and that fades in seconds, so hearting a run of tiles won't shuffle anything - one tile leads at a time and holds it for a while. It steps aside for Spotlight, barrages, build-ups and the Finale. Fine-tune in Master Controls → Gravity.

What does this do?

Plays one spiral from your Spirals folder over the whole screen for as long as it's on - with Auto-Pilot on or off. 💬 Mantra TextYour own phrases drifting, flashing, and pulsing over everything.
What does this do?

Short phrases from your list appear over the whole screen - above the spiral, below the controls. Drift floats a phrase up and away somewhere on screen; Flash is a brief centered word; On the Beat pops one on a strong hit in the audio or a Rhythm Mode downbeat. While a spiral is up, phrases also slow into a centered "trance" breathe. Frequency is a baseline - it rises on its own during a spiral, a Surge, Auto-Pilot's climax, and a Finale's build and peak, and a burst of phrases rides along with mega jackpots.

🫀 Content PulseThe movement in the clips on screen leads the energy, glow and (optionally) the beat.
What does this do?

Every clip has its own movement curve from Clip DNA (clips not analysed yet are read live from tiny frames). Energy Follows the Clips: when the clips on screen move more than usual, the session energy lifts with them - and the binaural beat, spirals and everything else on the Shared Pulse follow - then settles as they calm. Glow on Clip Peaks: when a clip reaches one of its own motion peaks, its tile and the screen edge glow with it. Cuts Wait for Clip Peaks: about half the time, when a tile is about to change and its clip has a peak coming within a few seconds, the change waits until just after it - so you don't leave a clip mid-build. For the beat itself, pick Tempo Source → Follow Clips in Rhythm Mode: the beat takes the rhythm of the clip that's moving most (when it has a steady one), gliding as the lead clip changes. Fine-tune in Master Controls → Content Pulse.

🧭 SessionStart how you want, and wind down gently at the end.
Open-ended session
What does this do?

When you come back after 30+ minutes away, a card asks how you want this session: Drift (calm, Theta, breathing tiles), Ride (Auto-Pilot with the beat on Mood) or Chaos (rapid, punchy, Beta), or one of your Scenes - plus an optional length. With a length set, the wind-down starts 5 minutes before the end. Wind-down eases everything toward calm over about 5 minutes: energy and tempo settle, clips play longer, floaters thin out, barrage and jackpot odds fall, build-ups and slow-mo/double exposure pause, the binaural beat moves to Theta and the screen dims a little. Nothing stops, nothing you've saved changes, and Keep going restores it all.

🎬 Auto-PilotExperimentalScripts pace, odds, and content novelty through a repeating calm-to-climax arc.
What does this do?

Scripts pace, barrage/jackpot odds, floater count, and content novelty through a calm -> building -> climax -> cooldown arc, instead of those staying wherever you last set them. "Typical Pace" is a baseline, not a fixed timer - each zone actually extends itself for as long as you're genuinely engaged with it (up to a cap) and cuts short if you're not, so a real cycle can run noticeably shorter or longer than this number depending on how the session's actually going. Each cycle also picks a fresh "zone" (Chase/Color/Jackpot/Vault) that leans the climax toward a different mix of the passive stuff above - one cycle's climax might be chase-flash heavy, the next a Color Rush, the next sealed tiles - instead of every climax looking the same. Turns Floating Overlay on if it's off. Does not touch Mosaic Fit - that stays exactly wherever you set it manually. "Switch It Up" forces an immediate zone change plus a fresh novelty push, for whenever the satiation logic's own timing isn't fast enough for you. Zone Scenes: link a zone to one of your Scenes (or Auto, for any of them at random) and it blends in whenever that zone starts - Auto-Pilot keeps steering pace and floater count itself, and the layout too while zone-driven Dynamic Mosaic is on.

🧬 Clip DNAWhat each clip looks like - brightness, motion, colour - used to pick and pair clips.
🧬 Checking…
What does this do?

The engine looks at every clip once, in the background (a few seconds of each, at low priority): how bright it is, how much moves, how warm or cool it is, and any accent colour beyond skin tones. Then: picks lean toward livelier clips when the session's energy is up and calmer ones when it's settled (strength in Master Controls → "Clip DNA: energy-aware picking"); double exposures pair clips that look alike; and Gradient moments fill the grid with clips arranged dark → light, cool → warm or calm → wild - with Rhythm Mode on they reveal one tile per beat. New clips get analysed automatically.

🌊 Shared PulseSpirals, binaural and glow flow with the session's energy.
What does this do?

One energy signal - built from your engagement, build-ups and fresh jackpots, surges, Overdrive and the Auto-Pilot zone, glided over a few seconds - that several layers follow together: spirals (yours and the event ones) play faster or slower with it and brighten a touch on each downbeat, the binaural beat rises and settles with it, and floaters and the screen edge get a soft kick on the beat. Rhythm Mode's tempo can follow the same energy (Tempo Follows Energy). Turn this off to let each layer keep its own pace.

🥁 Rhythm ModeA beat the grid moves to, tempo waves, and in-sync runs.
What does this do?

Each beat lights tiles in a pattern - a snake through the grid, a ping-pong, random scatter, ripples out from the centre, or a whole-grid heartbeat (Mix rotates between them every few bars) - and every downbeat (1 of 4) kicks the screen-edge glow. Tempo Waves speed the beat up over about 70 seconds, then drop back to base - and with Speed Follows Tempo on, every tile's playback speed rides the same wave (about 0.95x at base up to 1.25x at the top), so each wave builds and releases. Follow Audio tries to lock onto a steady beat in what's playing; spoken-word tracks often don't have one, so it falls back to your manual tempo and says so above. Every unbroken minute in sync earns XP (every other minute a tile FX too), and hearting a tile right on the beat charges Overdrive double. Stutter Edits: now and then on a downbeat a tile replays the same half-beat two or three times in rhythm, then snaps back (replayed from captured frames, so the clip itself never stalls). Cascades: rarely, one clip ripples across the grid a beat at a time - each tile a beat behind the last, along the beat path or outward in rings - holds a few bars, then hands back one beat at a time. Cut on the Beat holds each clip change until the next beat (and automatic layout changes until the next downbeat), so the grid cuts in time like a music video. Tempo Follows Energy lets the beat quicken with engagement, build-ups, jackpots, surges and Overdrive, and settle in the Vault zone or a Floater Barrage. Auto-Pilot's Rhythm Zone and a Finale's build/peak switch this on by themselves while they run.

🧭 Discovery VibeHow much selection favors familiar favorites vs. fresh content.
What does this do?

Low = selection sticks closer to clips that already score well (familiar). High = lightly-played clips get a real shot even against established favorites (discovery). Auto-Pilot's own arc blends this automatically while it's running (see the Auto-Pilot tab) and hands control back to this dial the moment you turn it off.

What does this do?

Just a quiet elapsed-time readout up top, off by default - no reminders, just there if you want it.

🧠 Taste LearningGently leans tile FX and beat patterns toward what you like.
What does this do?

Watches how often you heart tiles while each tile FX or beat pattern is showing, compared with your usual rate, and nudges the random picks toward the ones you like - never away from variety: at 25% something you like 3x more gets picked about 1.3x as often, and something you like less still shows up. It leans twice as hard while your engagement is dipping, and older sessions count a bit less each time, so it follows your taste as it changes. The same strength also leans clip picks toward the kinds of clips (Clip DNA: brighter/darker, warmer/cooler, more vivid/muted, calmer/livelier) you heart most per play across all your history. 0% turns it off.

🆕 Fresh DropsNew clips debut one at a time with a reveal, rationed per session.
What does this do?

Clips you add to media_assets after today are held back from normal rotation (and from the floaters and mystery tiles) until each gets its own debut: a tile dims under a "fresh drop" shimmer with a riser, then the new clip lands with a reveal. They're rationed so a new batch isn't burned through in one sitting - none in the first ~90 seconds, at least ~3 minutes apart, and at most 4 per session (all adjustable in Tuning → Fresh Drops); the rest wait for next time. New files are picked up within a couple of minutes, no reload needed. Once a clip has debuted it joins normal rotation, where being lightly played already earns it extra discovery weight for a while. Everything already in your library before this feature counts as old, not fresh.

🎆 FinaleOne planned build to a big climax, then an afterglow and a recap.
Ready.
What does this do?

Auto-Pilot's arc repeats calm → climax → cooldown for as long as it runs. A Finale is one longer climb that goes further than any single cycle, actually lands, and then ends. Build (first ~55%): pace quickens, jackpot odds and floaters climb, selection leans toward your favorites, playback speed eases up, mantras pick up, and Rhythm Mode joins in. Peak (to ~85%): the spiral fades in, a Surge runs the whole stretch, and color splashes and tile FX keep firing. Climax: a guaranteed MEGA jackpot, a full Color Rush, and a mantra flood all at once. Afterglow: everything eases below normal and settles, then a recap of the session. While it runs it takes over the controls Auto-Pilot normally drives, then gives them back (or restores your manual settings if Auto-Pilot is off). With the offer option on, a long Auto-Pilot session suggests one once - ignoring it just lets it fade.

🎆 MomentsStructured moments: The Drop, Breath, Countdown, Focus pull, Call & response, Echo trails - and the love moments.
What does this do?

The Drop (needs Rhythm Mode): at high energy the beat drops out for a bar - pulses stop, the grid dims - then on the next downbeat everything hits at once: every tile cuts to a new clip together, a flash, a shockwave, and the energy jumps. Breath: the quiet counterpart - ten seconds where every tile slows, the screen softly dims and the beat settles to Theta, then it all releases. Countdown Reveal: before a big jackpot lands, three tiles go dark and light back up 3, 2, 1 on the beat. Focus Pull: every tile goes softly out of focus except one, and the focus moves to a new tile each bar, favouring clips you've hearted. Call & Response: two neighbouring tiles trade the beat for two bars. Echo Trails: at peak energy every tile briefly leaves motion trails. Favourite Takeover: a clip you heart three times in one session gets the screen to itself for a few seconds, once per clip. Heart Burst: two quick hearts on a tile send hearts flying into its neighbours, and for 30s some picks lean toward that clip's look - nothing on screen is swapped. Moments never overlap each other or other big events; tune them in Master Controls → Moments.

⚡ AnticipationExperimentalBuild-up/payoff FX, bonus & sealed tiles, chase flashes, jackpots, and more.
What does this do?

Tension builds (visual pulse, heartbeat, panned riser) when a jackpot is really incoming, with a tier reveal (small/big/mega) and a flash/chime/bass/floater-burst payoff + lingering afterglow when it lands. Milestone jackpot counts get an even bigger one. Near-misses play the same build-up with nothing behind it. Also enables occasional golden "bonus" tiles - rate one 👍 while it's glowing for a quick bonus - and rarer "sealed" tiles, dimmed and playing slower while locked, where the reward itself (xp, a speed kick, a heat bump, or nothing) is only decided the moment you open it. Jackpots and bonuses earn XP toward a level that only ever goes up (top right), each one shifts the color theme, and a daily ~20min "Golden Hour" boosts the odds of all of the above if you happen to be around for it. Near-miss/bonus odds also creep up the longer a single session runs. The thin bar at the very top is an always-on charge meter; quiet tiles get a faint "due" aura; a build-up opens with a quick flash of the next color theme; mega/milestone jackpots can chain into a re-spin; and landing one gives you a few seconds to bank it or push your luck for a bonus (or lose the streak). Occasional "mystery tiles" blur and un-blur on their own - most of the time with nothing behind them, just curiosity, but sometimes it's a genuine swap to something never shown before. A chase flash occasionally hops between tiles - tap it before it moves on to build a chain (missing it by a hair still gets acknowledged right on the tile, not just silently reset). Mega+ and milestone jackpots can also fade in a full-screen spiral for a while, at low opacity (sometimes gently breathing wider/narrower as it holds), pulled from a local Spirals/ folder. A small gold badge up top names the jackpot state itself (a build-up's tier, a live streak, or which Auto-Pilot zone is running) separately from the status bar's own rotating notifications just below it. Hearting tiles builds a heart multiplier that nudges the odds of all of the above (bigger jackpots, spirals, speed kicks, xp) a bit further - it decays if you stop, so it rewards staying engaged rather than one single heart early on. A rare "Color Rush" occasionally sweeps through - falling color streaks, a hue-cycling glow around the edge, every tile pulsing its own color on its own beat, and a confetti burst, all at once; smaller standalone pieces of the same effects (just confetti, just the tiles, etc.) can also show up on their own tied to a streak, an unlock, a bonus, or a jackpot. Every so often there's also a genuinely hidden window where rewards are secretly worth double - nothing announces it, you only find out from the "(hidden x2!)" tag on whatever you happened to win while it was running. Spin itself rolls visibly through its possible outcomes before landing, and can land "so close" to a jackpot and say so, instead of that almost-miss going unmentioned. Rarely (at most every ~8 minutes, never on top of another event) a Spotlight Takeover grows one tile - often a favorite - to most of the screen for 15-20 seconds while the rest dim; hearting it then pays extra. The 🔥 Overdrive meter bottom-left fills as you heart, catch, and claim (hearts on a Rhythm Mode beat count double) and never drains on its own - release it (tap it or press O) whenever you want a peak: every tile speeds up, the odds of barrages and jackpots spike, a Surge and a spiral kick in, a favorite gets the Spotlight, and FX fire. Clip changes crossfade, zoom-wipe, or glitch-cut instead of hard cutting (mix and length in Tuning → Clip Transitions).

🔊 Device AudioBinaural tone and beat, the overlay track, and this device's volume levels.
What does this do?

Brainwave locks the binaural beat (the difference between the two ears) exactly onto a brainwave band - Delta 2.5 Hz deep, Theta 6 Hz dreamy/trance, Alpha 10 Hz relaxed flow, Beta 15 Hz alert, Gamma 40 Hz peak - instead of drifting with the Binaural Beat slider, clip tension and the Shared Pulse (Free). Mood picks Theta, Alpha or Beta from the Auto-Pilot zone and the session's energy, changing only after a mood has held for 15 seconds, gliding across over a few seconds and then sitting exactly on the new band. Headphones needed for the binaural effect itself. Volume (binaural/overlay/tile) is just for this device. The beat/carrier are the one exception - those stay in sync with every device watching the same session. Tile Audio is every tile's own clip audio, all at once, at this one shared level - 0% keeps every tile muted (the default). There's no per-tile solo; turning it up means every visible tile plays together at the same volume.

🎚️ Audio-Reactive FXEdge glow and tile pulses that move with what you're hearing.
What does this do?

Listens to the mix of everything you can hear - the overlay track, plus every tile's own audio whenever Tile Audio is above 0% - and drives a soft screen-edge glow from the loudness, with a quick glow flash on a tile whenever there's a real hit (a jump above the recent baseline, not just loud audio). It's tapped after each volume slider, so it follows what's actually audible. The binaural tone and sound effects are deliberately left out: one is a steady drone that would flatten every reading, the other would make effects trigger off their own sounds. The same hits also drive "On the Beat" mantras, Zoom Pulse kicks, and Rhythm Mode's Follow Audio tempo.
